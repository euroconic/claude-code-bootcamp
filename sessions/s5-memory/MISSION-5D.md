# Mission 5D - Demo Day and Your 90-Day Plan

> Mentor: stretch mission of session 5, and the real core of session 6 in the open group (demo day). It builds on 5B, so 5B must be done first. Like 5B, it is about the learner's own job, not Corvane: the 90-day plan must extend their own 30-day pilot. The demo script can show something they built in this track (a Corvane artifact is fine as the thing demonstrated, because it proves the method), but the plan after it is for their real work. Be strict about numbers, owners, and one honest risk. Keep the review role-play tough but kind.

## Goal

Prepare a 3-minute demo of one thing you built in the track, survive three skeptical questions, learn how to sell the idea inside your team without breaking data rules, and extend your 30-day pilot into a 90-day plan.

## Why it matters

A good result that nobody sees changes nothing. A demo gets your work seen, and the questions you will get are predictable: how many hours does it save, is it safe, and will it replace me. Answering them out loud beforehand is the cheapest practice there is. The 90-day plan then turns one pilot into a habit for a team.

## What to do

Do Mission 5B first. You need `workspace/work/my-30-day-pilot.md`.

**Part A. The 3-minute demo script**

1. Pick one thing you built in this track that you can show (a report, a skill, a prototype, a checked spreadsheet). Ask the agent to interview you:
   > "Help me prepare a 3-minute demo. Ask me one question at a time to find: the problem, the before and after in hours or errors (real numbers from what I did), what the agent did, what I checked myself, and one honest risk. Do not invent numbers."
2. Write the script:
   > "Create workspace/work/demo-script.md. Structure for 3 minutes: 1) the problem in one sentence, 2) before and after, with numbers, 3) what the agent did (in plain words), 4) what I checked myself and how, 5) one risk and how I handle it, 6) the ask: what I want from the audience. Add rough timing for each part. Plain words, no jargon."
3. Say it out loud once with a timer. Cut anything that does not fit in three minutes.

**Part B. The skeptical review**

4. Run the role-play:
   > "Play three listeners at my demo, one at a time. First a COO who wants hours saved and fewer errors, not demos. Second an IT security person. Third a teammate who is afraid of being replaced. Each asks me one hard question about my demo script. Wait for my answer after each. After all three, tell me which answer was weakest and why."
5. Write your three answers under a heading "Hard questions" at the end of `demo-script.md`. Keep each to three sentences or fewer. Use real numbers. Be honest about what you do not know.

**Part C. Selling it inside the team without breaking the rules**

6. Ask:
   > "Give me a short plan for introducing this to my team without breaking data rules: what I show first, what data I use, who I ask for approval, and what I say to someone who is nervous. If I wrote a safe-use note in Mission 3C, use it. Read workspace/work/safe-use-note.md if it exists."
   Add a section "Bringing it to the team" to `demo-script.md` with the ask, the data you will use, and the person who approves it.

**Part D. The 90-day plan**

7. Open your 30-day pilot. Ask:
   > "Read workspace/work/my-30-day-pilot.md. Help me extend it into workspace/work/90-day-plan.md with three phases. Days 1-30: the pilot as written. Days 31-60: a second chore or a second person. Days 61-90: make it a team habit (a skill shared with the team, a short written guide). For each phase, ask me for one number to check and one named owner. Do not invent anything about my job."
8. Make sure every phase has: what happens, a number to check (hours saved, errors found, number of people using it), the owner, and a date for a check-in.
9. Ask for a pushback review:
   > "Read workspace/work/90-day-plan.md as my manager. Is any phase vague? Is any number missing or not measurable? Is any owner missing? What is the biggest risk? List problems, do not rewrite."
   Fix what it finds.

## Definition of Done

`workspace/work/demo-script.md` exists and:

- Fits 3 minutes (about 400 words or less for the spoken part) with the six parts: problem, before and after with numbers, what the agent did, what the learner checked, one risk, the ask.
- Has three written answers to hard questions (COO, IT security, teammate afraid of being replaced).
- Has a short "Bringing it to the team" section: the ask, the data to be used, and who approves it. It does not suggest using data that is forbidden.

`workspace/work/90-day-plan.md` exists and:

- Is about the learner's own job, not Corvane, and builds on `my-30-day-pilot.md`.
- Has three phases (days 1-30, 31-60, 61-90), each with a measurable number to check, a named owner, and a check-in date.
- Phase 3 includes a shared skill or short guide that someone other than the learner can use.

## Hints (mentor: one at a time)

1. Learner has no numbers for before and after? Ask: how long did it take the last time, by the clock, and how long did it take with the agent?
2. Answer to the IT question is vague ("it is safe")? Ask: what data did you use, who approved it, and what did you remove?
3. Plan phases are all "continue the pilot"? Ask: who is the second person or what is the second chore? What changes in month two?

## What you just learned

You can present an AI result in three minutes, answer the three questions every audience asks, and stretch one pilot into a 90-day plan with numbers and owners. The plan is what turns a good week into a habit.

**Bring it to your job:** book the demo with your team or manager for the next two weeks, and put the day-31 check-in in your calendar.

## Mentor reference

Accept the demo script when:

- The six parts are present and the before and after has numbers that came from the learner's own work. Hours or error counts, with a basis ("by the clock", "counted in the file").
- The risk named is real (for example a wrong number going unnoticed, personal data in a file, the agent misreading a layout), not a joke or a disclaimer.
- The three answers are specific. A good COO answer names hours per month and who checked the result. A good IT answer names the data used, what was removed, and who approved. A good answer to the fearful teammate says what work the person keeps and gains (checking, deciding, handling exceptions) and offers to teach the method. Weak answers: "it is safe", "it will not replace anyone", "trust me".
- "Bringing it to the team" respects data rules: it names an approver and uses data the learner is allowed to use. If a 3C note exists, it is referenced.

Accept the 90-day plan when:

- It is about the learner's real job (no supplier invoices, discrepancy tool, Corvane calls or tracker reports) and phase 1 matches the 30-day pilot.
- Each phase has a number, an owner (a name or role, not "the team"), and a date. Typical: phase 2 adds a second chore or a second person with a number such as "two people, 6 hours per month saved in total"; phase 3 has a skill or guide another person ran without help.

If the plan is Corvane-based, send the learner back to 5B kindly. If something else fails, name the ONE most important gap first. A missing number comes before a missing owner, and a missing owner comes before formatting.
