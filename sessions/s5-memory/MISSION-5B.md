# Mission 5B - Your Own 30-Day Pilot

> Mentor: this is the point of the whole Work track. The learner leaves Corvane and plans a real pilot on their own job. Do NOT accept a Corvane chore (invoices, the discrepancy tool, Corvane meetings, tracker reports). If they suggest one, say kindly: that was practice, now pick something from your own week. Be strict about numbers, a real file type, a safety line, and a dated first step. Vague pilots die in week two.

## Goal

Fill in a one-page pilot card for one real chore from your own job, with hours, files, a success metric, a data safety check, and a first step with a date.

## Why it matters

Skills fade if you do not use them on real work. A pilot is a small, time-boxed experiment: one chore, 30 days, a number to beat. It is easy to start, easy to stop, and it gives you something to show your manager.

## What to do

1. Open `data/corvane/s5/pilot-card-template.md` and skim the nine sections.
2. Brainstorm with the agent. Do not describe Corvane, describe your week:
   > "I will list the repeated tasks in my own job. Ask me questions one at a time until we find one chore that happens at least twice a month, takes more than an hour each time, and starts from a file or a text I already have. Then help me pick the best one."
3. Pick one chore. If you have two, take the one with the clearer input file.
4. Fill the card:
   > "Copy data/corvane/s5/pilot-card-template.md to workspace/work/my-30-day-pilot.md and fill it with me, one section at a time. Ask me for every number and do not guess. If I am vague, push back."
5. Do the math on hours per month with the agent. Hours each time x times per month. Write the calculation.
6. Fill the data safety section honestly. What is in those files that must not go to an agent: names of private persons, bank details, contracts, salary data, anything your company forbids? Decide how you will remove or replace it, and who you will ask if unsure.
7. Write the first step so small that you cannot avoid it: 30 minutes, one file, one date in your calendar. Then add the three check-in dates (day 7, 14, 30).
8. Ask for a pushback review:
   > "Read workspace/work/my-30-day-pilot.md as a skeptical manager. What is vague? What number is missing? Is the success metric measurable? Do not rewrite it, just list the problems."
   Fix them.

## Definition of Done

`workspace/work/my-30-day-pilot.md` exists and:

- The chore is from the learner's real job, not Corvane.
- It has hours per month with the calculation shown (for example 3 hours x 4 times = 12 hours per month).
- It names a real input file type (Excel export, PDF, transcript, email thread) and one real example file name.
- It has a success metric with a number.
- The data safety line says what must not go to the agent and how it will be handled.
- The first step is 30 minutes or less and has a calendar date. Check-in dates for day 7, 14, and 30 are filled in.

## Hints (mentor: one at a time)

1. Stuck on picking a chore? Ask: what task do you dread on Monday mornings, or do again and again with copy and paste?
2. Metric too vague ("faster", "better")? Ask: what would you measure with a stopwatch or by counting?
3. Learner offers a Corvane chore or a one-off task? Ask: will this happen again next month? Does it start from a file you can actually get? If either answer is no, pick another.

## What you just learned

A good pilot is small and concrete: one chore, a number, a file, a safety line, and a date. You now have a plan you can start this week, with check-in dates that keep you honest.

**Bring it to your job:** do the first step on the date you wrote, and put the day-7 check-in in your calendar right now.

## Mentor reference

There is no single right answer. Accept the card only when ALL of these are true:

- Not a Corvane chore (no supplier invoices, discrepancy tool, Corvane calls, tracker reports).
- Hours per month stated with a calculation. Typical good range: 4 to 20 hours per month.
- Input file type is real and named (for example "weekly sales export in xlsx", "recorded call transcript in txt").
- Success metric contains a number and a way to measure it.
- Data safety line names at least one kind of data to keep out, or states plainly that the files contain nothing sensitive and why.
- First step is dated and fits in 30 minutes. Three check-in dates exist and are in the future relative to the first step.

Example of a card that passes (illustrative, not to be copied): chore "monthly status deck summary from three project spreadsheets", 2 people, 3 hours x 1 per month for each = 6 hours per month, input xlsx, success metric "draft ready in 45 minutes instead of 3 hours, with zero wrong numbers on a manual check", safety "remove names of external contractors, ask IT security if unsure", first step "Thursday 20 November, 30 minutes, one anonymized spreadsheet".

If the card fails, tell the learner the ONE most important gap first.
